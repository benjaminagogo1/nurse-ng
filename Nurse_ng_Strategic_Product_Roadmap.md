# Strategic Product Proposal: Nurse.ng
## Verified Professional Infrastructure for Nurses in Nigeria and Beyond

**Document type:** Engineering and product strategy proposal  
**Audience:** Engineering team, product leadership, nursing stakeholders, employers, education partners, and potential funders  
**Status:** Proposal for technical review and staged validation  
**Geographic starting point:** Nigeria; designed for possible international expansion  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Important:** This document defines a strategic product direction and a proposed roadmap. It is not evidence of regulatory approval, market demand, signed partnerships, or a commitment to build every proposed feature.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Nurse.ng is proposed as a trusted digital professional platform for nurses, beginning in Nigeria. Its purpose is to help nurses establish a credible professional presence, discover legitimate career opportunities, access professional development, and connect with peers and employers. The platform should build on—rather than impersonate or replace—the official registration and verification functions of the Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria (NMCN).

The central product hypothesis is that nurses and employers need a more useful professional layer than a static directory or a general social network: one that makes credentials and employment status easier to present and check, helps employers discover suitable candidates, supports career development, and reduces friction in local and international career mobility.

The initial product should not attempt to become a regulator, a licensing authority, a recruitment agency in every jurisdiction, a learning-management system, and a social network all at once. It should prove a narrow, trustworthy workflow first:

1. A nurse creates an account and a professional profile.
2. The nurse submits the minimum information needed to establish identity and professional status.
3. Verification is completed only through lawful, reliable sources and documented review procedures.
4. The nurse controls what profile information is public or shared with employers.
5. An employer or recruiter with an approved account searches for candidates and contacts them through controlled workflows.
6. The platform records verification status, employer actions, reports, and disputes.

**Proposed first release:** verified nurse profiles and a basic employer directory/search workflow, supported by manual review where automated verification is not officially available. Jobs, continuing professional development, peer networking, and international mobility should be staged after trust, user demand, and operating economics are demonstrated.

The long-term vision is to become professional infrastructure for nurses: identity and credential presentation, career opportunities, professional learning, community, and mobility—without claiming that a platform badge is itself a professional licence.

## 2. Product Vision, Mission, and Positioning

### Vision
A trusted professional digital home where nurses can present their verified credentials, grow their careers, and connect to legitimate opportunities.

### Mission
Reduce friction and uncertainty in professional discovery and career progression for nurses by providing trustworthy profiles, transparent verification, relevant opportunities, and useful professional services.

### Positioning statement
Nurse.ng is a nurse-focused professional platform that helps nurses present their credentials and connect with legitimate employers and professional opportunities. Official licensing remains the responsibility of the relevant regulator.

### What Nurse.ng is
- A professional profile and discovery platform.
- A credential-verification workflow where lawful source access and human review support it.
- A jobs and career opportunity marketplace, once employer quality controls exist.
- A professional community and learning-discovery layer, introduced in stages.
- A potential bridge to international career information and recruitment pathways, subject to destination-country requirements.

### What Nurse.ng is not
- Not the NMCN or a substitute for its register.
- Not a guarantee of employment, competence, immigration eligibility, or licence transfer.
- Not an unmoderated repository for sensitive patient information.
- Not an agency authorized to issue, suspend, or revoke professional licences.
- Not a promise that all credentials can be verified automatically.
- Not a global recruitment service at launch.

## 3. Problem Statement and Hypotheses

### Problems to investigate
1. Nurses may lack a well-organized, portable professional profile that can be shared with employers.
2. Employers may spend time checking identity, qualifications, registration status, experience, and references across fragmented channels.
3. Legitimate job opportunities may be difficult to distinguish from misleading, fraudulent, or exploitative offers.
4. Nurses may struggle to find relevant professional development, mentorship, and peer connections.
5. International career pathways can be confusing because requirements vary by destination, regulator, employer, and immigration route.
6. Generic professional networks may not provide nurse-specific verification, workflows, or local context.

These are **problem hypotheses**, not proven market facts. They must be validated through interviews and workflow observation before significant implementation spending.

### Primary hypotheses
- Nurses will create and maintain profiles if the platform gives them tangible value and does not expose unnecessary personal data.
- Employers will pay for reliable candidate discovery or workflow tools if they reduce recruitment time or verification effort.
- Nurses will trust the service only if verification claims are precise, transparent, and appealable.
- Job listings with employer verification and reporting controls will be more valuable than an unmoderated job board.
- Professional learning and community features can improve retention, but should not distract from the core trust-and-career workflow.

### Discovery questions
- How do nurses currently prove registration and qualifications to employers?
- What are the most time-consuming steps in hiring nurses?
- Which sources can be lawfully and reliably used to verify professional status?
- What would nurses refuse to upload or make public?
- What are the most common job scams or recruitment abuses?
- Which employer segment has a clear budget and urgent need?
- Would users prefer free profiles with paid employer services, a subscription, or another model?
- What would make nurses return monthly rather than only when seeking a job?

## 4. Users, Customers, and Stakeholders

### Primary users
- Student nurses and newly qualified nurses (with status clearly distinguished from registered professionals).
- Registered nurses seeking employment or career progression.
- Experienced nurses seeking leadership, specialization, mentorship, or international opportunities.
- Nurse educators and professional mentors.

### Customers and payers
- Hospitals and clinics.
- Home-care providers and other legitimate healthcare employers.
- Recruitment firms that pass platform vetting.
- Training providers and continuing professional development (CPD) partners, where permitted.
- Potentially institutions or professional associations purchasing organizational services.

Users and payers are not always the same. Nurses should not be forced to pay merely to make a basic professional profile discoverable if the business model can be supported by employer-side services.

### Stakeholders
- NMCN and other relevant professional bodies, as applicable.
- Employers and human-resource teams.
- Nursing schools and training institutions.
- Data protection and consumer-protection authorities.
- Recruitment and education partners.
- Nurses' associations and professional communities.

**Engagement rule:** do not imply endorsement by any regulator, institution, or association without written authorization.

## 5. Value Proposition and Differentiation

### For nurses
- A reusable professional profile.
- Clear distinction between self-declared information and verified information.
- Control over profile visibility and employer contact.
- Access to vetted opportunities and practical career information.
- Reporting, appeal, and account-support mechanisms.

### For employers
- Searchable profiles with explicit verification labels and dates.
- Candidate contact workflows with consent and abuse controls.
- Employer accounts with organization checks and accountable users.
- Structured shortlists and candidate communication.
- Reduced administrative friction, without promising that a profile alone proves clinical competence.

### Potential differentiation
- Nurse-specific workflows and terminology.
- Transparent evidence labels: self-declared, document reviewed, source-confirmed, expired, disputed, or not verified.
- Nigerian context and low-bandwidth usability.
- Strong anti-scam controls and employer accountability.
- Career pathways that distinguish Nigerian registration from requirements in other countries.
- Privacy-first profile controls.
- Partnerships that improve trust without pretending to have regulatory authority.

Differentiation must be tested against existing directories, professional networks, job boards, recruitment firms, and official registers. A competitor's existence does not invalidate the idea; it means the team must identify a specific underserved workflow and prove it can serve that workflow better.

## 6. Product Principles

1. **Truthful verification:** never use “verified” without defining what was checked, by whom, when, and against which source.
2. **Regulatory respect:** official licensing decisions remain with the relevant regulator.
3. **Privacy by design:** collect the minimum necessary data and make visibility understandable.
4. **Safety from exploitation:** actively detect and respond to fraudulent employers, misleading listings, and recruitment abuse.
5. **User agency:** provide access, correction, export, deletion where legally appropriate, and appeal processes.
6. **Accessibility:** design for mobile devices, low bandwidth, and varied digital literacy.
7. **Evidence before expansion:** new feature families require proof of demand, operational capacity, and sustainable economics.
8. **No pay-to-fake trust:** paid placement must never alter credential-verification outcomes.
9. **Explainable status:** show limitations and expiry dates instead of presenting a permanent green badge.
10. **Operational accountability:** every high-impact review or moderation action must be attributable and auditable.

## 7. Scope: MVP, Later Releases, and Explicit Exclusions

### MVP: prove trust and professional discovery
- Account creation and secure sign-in.
- Nurse profile with structured professional history.
- Explicit professional status categories (for example, student, newly qualified, registered, specialist where recognized).
- Credential evidence submission with data minimization.
- Manual verification queue and reviewer audit trail.
- Public/private field controls.
- Employer organization registration and review.
- Basic candidate search, subject to consent and privacy settings.
- Contact/request-to-connect workflow.
- Reporting, blocking, appeals, and support process.
- Administrative dashboard for verification and abuse cases.
- Basic analytics that avoid exposing sensitive individual data.

### Release 2: jobs marketplace
- Employer job creation and review.
- Job categories, location, qualifications, work type, and closing date.
- Candidate applications or controlled contact requests.
- Employer reporting and moderation.
- Scam patterns, duplicate listing detection, and takedown workflow.
- Clear terms on fees and no guarantee of employment.

### Release 3: professional development and community
- CPD and course directory.
- Mentorship discovery and professional groups.
- Events and announcements.
- Moderation, conflict-of-interest, and promotional-content rules.
- Partner integrations only after due diligence.

### Release 4: international mobility support
- Country-specific information pages reviewed by qualified partners.
- Destination regulator links and checklists.
- Distinguish information from legal, immigration, or licensing advice.
- Employer/recruiter workflows with destination-specific compliance checks.
- No “guaranteed relocation” claims.

### Explicitly out of scope for early releases
- Issuing licences, certificates, or regulator-equivalent credentials.
- Storing patient records.
- Automated final judgments about a nurse's competence.
- Unmoderated direct messaging at scale.
- Automated cross-border eligibility determinations.
- Charging nurses for access to their own submitted data.
- Selling personal data or allowing hidden profile scraping.

## 8. Core User Journeys

### Journey A: Nurse onboarding
1. User selects a clear status category.
2. User creates an account and verifies control of their contact method.
3. User completes a profile with only required fields.
4. User sees a plain-language explanation of verification and privacy.
5. User submits evidence through a secure upload flow.
6. System marks the record as pending; it must not imply verification while review is incomplete.
7. Authorized reviewer checks evidence using an approved procedure.
8. Reviewer records evidence source, decision, date, and limitations.
9. Nurse is notified and can correct information or appeal.
10. Profile displays only fields permitted by the nurse and platform policy.

### Journey B: Employer onboarding
1. Organization representative creates an employer account.
2. Platform verifies organization identity and the representative's authority.
3. High-risk or suspicious organizations enter manual review.
4. Employer accepts rules on data use, anti-discrimination, and no misleading recruitment claims.
5. Authorized employer users receive scoped permissions.
6. Access and candidate-contact actions are logged.
7. Reports trigger review and possible suspension.

### Journey C: Candidate discovery
1. Employer searches by appropriate professional criteria.
2. Search returns only profiles that are discoverable under their settings.
3. Verification label and date are visible with limitations.
4. Employer requests contact or uses an approved communication flow.
5. Nurse can accept, decline, block, or report.
6. Platform monitors suspicious bulk access and scraping.

### Journey D: Job listing
1. Employer submits a structured vacancy.
2. Platform validates required fields and reviews the listing.
3. Listing becomes visible only after checks pass.
4. Nurse reviews the employer and job details.
5. Applications/contact requests are recorded.
6. Listing expires or is closed; reports remain auditable.
7. Abuse cases can trigger delisting, warnings, suspension, or escalation.

## 9. Verification and Trust Framework

Verification is the product's highest-risk promise. The team must publish a precise verification taxonomy.

### Suggested labels
- **Self-declared:** supplied by the user and not independently checked.
- **Document reviewed:** a reviewer inspected a submitted document; this does not necessarily prove it is authentic.
- **Source-confirmed:** checked against an identified, lawfully accessible authoritative source.
- **Expired / needs renewal:** prior evidence is no longer current for the stated purpose.
- **Unable to verify:** source unavailable or evidence insufficient.
- **Under review / disputed:** a question or appeal is open.
- **Revoked / invalidated:** use only when a reliable source and lawful process support the status; never infer revocation from silence.

### Required controls
- Record who reviewed a claim and when.
- Store the minimum evidence needed and restrict access.
- Define retention periods and deletion rules.
- Use dual review for high-impact or disputed cases.
- Provide correction and appeal workflows.
- Prevent paid customers from changing verification outcomes.
- Re-check status at a risk-appropriate interval when lawful sources permit.
- Clearly distinguish NMCN registration from foreign licensing, specialty recognition, or employment eligibility.

### Failure scenarios
- Official source is unavailable.
- Names differ across documents.
- Document is altered or stolen.
- A legitimate nurse's account is impersonated.
- A profile is reported maliciously.
- Reviewer makes a mistake.
- Registration status changes after verification.
- A recruiter attempts to scrape profiles.
- An employer asks for discriminatory or unlawful filtering.

The platform must fail transparently: show “unable to verify” rather than invent certainty, and provide a controlled human review path.

## 10. Functional Requirements

### Identity and access
- Secure account registration and recovery.
- Multi-factor authentication for administrators and privileged reviewers.
- Role-based access control for nurse, employer user, reviewer, moderator, and administrator.
- Session revocation and suspicious-login monitoring.
- Rate limits for authentication and high-risk actions.

### Profiles
- Structured fields with provenance (user-provided vs reviewed).
- Field-level visibility settings.
- Change history for material credential claims.
- Profile completeness indicator that does not imply competence.
- Export and correction pathways.

### Verification
- Evidence upload with type and size limits.
- Malware scanning and safe file handling.
- Reviewer queue, assignment, decision, reason, and audit history.
- Expiry/recheck workflows where relevant.
- Appeals and dispute resolution.

### Search and recruitment
- Permission-aware search.
- Anti-scraping and rate limiting.
- Candidate contact requests with consent.
- Employer organization verification.
- Job listing review, expiry, and reporting.
- Audit trail for bulk access and exports.

### Administration and support
- Case management for reports, appeals, suspected fraud, and data requests.
- Moderation decisions with reason codes.
- Account suspension and reinstatement workflows.
- Support escalation and incident management.

### Non-functional requirements
- Mobile-first and usable on low-bandwidth connections.
- Accessibility aligned with WCAG 2.2 AA as a target.
- Clear availability and recovery objectives set before production.
- Structured logs and monitoring without logging passwords, authentication tokens, or unnecessary sensitive documents.
- Automated backups and tested restoration.
- Data export, retention, and deletion controls.
- Security testing before public launch.

## 11. Proposed Technical Architecture

The team should select technologies after documenting constraints, team skills, cost, hosting options, and expected scale. The architecture below is logical, not a mandate to adopt a particular framework.

### Logical components
1. **Web client:** responsive nurse and employer experiences.
2. **API layer:** validated endpoints, authentication, authorization, rate limits, versioning.
3. **Identity and access module:** accounts, sessions, roles, organization membership.
4. **Profile domain:** professional profile, field visibility, history.
5. **Verification service:** submissions, review cases, evidence metadata, decisions.
6. **Employer and jobs domain:** organization checks, listings, applications/contact requests.
7. **Moderation/case management:** reports, appeals, suspensions, fraud investigations.
8. **Notification service:** email/SMS or other channels selected for cost and reliability; avoid sensitive content in notifications.
9. **Relational database:** transactional records and constraints.
10. **Private object storage:** evidence documents, with strict access and retention policies.
11. **Audit/event trail:** append-oriented records for high-impact actions.
12. **Observability:** metrics, structured logs, traces where appropriate, alerts.
13. **Background jobs:** scanning, notifications, expiry checks, moderation signals.

### Architecture principles
- Keep business rules out of UI components.
- Enforce authorization on the server, not only in the interface.
- Use database constraints for invariants that must always hold.
- Separate public profile fields from private verification evidence.
- Store secrets outside source control.
- Use migrations and repeatable environment setup.
- Use explicit API schemas and versioning.
- Treat uploaded files as hostile input.
- Do not log sensitive evidence or credentials.
- Avoid premature microservices; a well-modularized application may be the right initial deployment.
- Keep the ability to split components later where scaling or organizational boundaries justify it.

### Illustrative core entities
- User
- NurseProfile
- CredentialClaim
- VerificationCase
- VerificationEvidenceMetadata
- VerificationDecision
- EmployerOrganization
- EmployerMembership
- JobListing
- ApplicationOrContactRequest
- ReportCase
- AppealCase
- ConsentOrVisibilityPreference
- AuditEvent
- NotificationDelivery

Use stable internal identifiers. Avoid making national identity numbers or professional registration numbers public keys. Define uniqueness and matching rules carefully; name alone is not a reliable identity key.

## 12. API and Data Design Expectations

- Use documented request/response schemas and consistent error formats.
- Validate all inputs on the server.
- Apply object-level authorization to every profile, evidence, application, and employer resource.
- Use pagination and bounded search queries.
- Use idempotency keys for operations where retries could create duplicate submissions.
- Keep privileged state transitions explicit and auditable.
- Use UTC timestamps internally and render local time clearly.
- Define data classification for every field: public, user-private, employer-shared, reviewer-only, or restricted.
- Establish retention and deletion policies before collecting evidence at scale.
- Do not expose internal database identifiers or evidence storage paths unnecessarily.
- Maintain an API contract test suite and authorization tests.

## 13. Security, Privacy, and Legal Readiness

The platform may process identity information, professional records, contact details, employment history, and sensitive documents. Nigerian legal obligations and any destination-country obligations must be assessed by qualified counsel and privacy professionals before launch.

### Minimum readiness work
- Map all personal data and purposes.
- Identify the data controller/processor roles and relevant legal bases.
- Publish understandable privacy notices and terms.
- Assess whether a Data Protection Impact Assessment is required or prudent.
- Establish access, correction, objection, deletion, and complaint handling as applicable.
- Sign appropriate data-processing agreements with vendors.
- Review hosting region, cross-border transfers, subprocessors, and backups.
- Define breach detection, escalation, notification, and evidence-preservation procedures.
- Set retention schedules and delete data that is no longer needed.
- Review employment, consumer-protection, advertising, and recruitment rules.
- Obtain legal advice before offering cross-border recruitment or immigration-related services.

### Security baseline
- TLS in transit and encryption at rest where supported.
- Strong password hashing using a maintained password-hashing algorithm.
- Multi-factor authentication for privileged roles.
- Least-privilege access and periodic access reviews.
- Secure file uploads and malware scanning.
- Dependency scanning, secret scanning, static analysis, and security review.
- Rate limiting and abuse detection.
- Tested backup restoration.
- Incident response playbook and named owners.
- Independent penetration testing before a broad public launch.

Do not claim “fully compliant” merely because a checklist exists. Compliance depends on actual operations, contracts, evidence, and legal interpretation.

## 14. Abuse, Fairness, and Safety

Threats include fake nurses, fake employers, credential fraud, recruitment scams, profile scraping, harassment, discriminatory filtering, doxxing, insider misuse, account takeover, and false accusations.

Controls:
- Employer verification and ongoing re-review.
- Nurse-controlled visibility and contact permissions.
- Reporting, blocking, appeals, and documented enforcement.
- Rate limits and anomaly detection.
- No public display of unnecessary identity documents or home addresses.
- Clear job advertising standards and prohibited-fee disclosures.
- Audit logs for access to restricted data.
- Fairness review of search and ranking features.
- No automated rejection or ranking based on sensitive attributes.
- Human review for high-impact account restrictions and disputes.
- A response process for urgent safety concerns.

## 15. Business Model and Unit Economics

Potential models to test:
1. **Employer subscription:** candidate search, team seats, shortlist tools, and recruitment workflow.
2. **Paid job listings:** transparent pricing with fraud screening; payment never influences verification.
3. **Institutional plans:** nursing schools, hospitals, or associations use profile or career-support tools.
4. **Training partnerships:** clearly disclosed referral or listing fees where lawful and ethical.
5. **Premium optional services for nurses:** only if they add genuine value and do not gate basic access to their own profile or exploit job-seeking urgency.

### Do not assume willingness to pay
Conduct pricing interviews and small paid pilots. Measure the full cost of verification review, support, moderation, storage, messaging, fraud investigation, and customer acquisition.

### Unit economics to measure
- Cost per verified profile.
- Minutes and labor cost per review.
- Employer acquisition cost.
- Paid employer conversion.
- Revenue per paying employer.
- Support/moderation cost per active user.
- Job-listing fraud rate and refund cost.
- Retention and repeat hiring.
- Gross margin after verification and support operations.

Do not scale paid acquisition until there is evidence of retention and a repeatable customer acquisition path.

## 16. Go-to-Market and Adoption

### Initial segment
Select one segment where trust and workflow pain can be tested with direct access—for example, a defined group of nurses and a small set of hospitals or home-care employers. Do not launch to every nurse and every employer simultaneously.

### Pilot design
- Recruit a small, diverse group of nurses.
- Secure written participation from a limited number of employers.
- Test onboarding, profile completion, verification, search, and contact requests.
- Manually support the process and record the time required.
- Interview users after each completed workflow.
- Track scams, false positives, verification disputes, and drop-off points.
- Publish no unsupported claims about coverage or accuracy.

### Adoption channels to evaluate
- Nursing communities and professional associations.
- Nursing schools and alumni networks.
- Employer HR teams.
- Career events and professional development partners.
- Referral incentives only if they do not encourage fake accounts or low-quality recruitment.

### Trust before growth
A small verified community with legitimate employers is more valuable than a large directory full of unverified profiles and stale vacancies.

## 17. Phased Roadmap and Exit Gates

### Phase 0 — Discovery and governance (Weeks 1–4; indicative)
**Work:** interviews, workflow observation, competitor review, legal/privacy assessment, source-verification feasibility, initial pricing tests, threat modelling.  
**Deliverables:** problem brief, user journeys, data map, verification policy draft, product requirements, risk register, pilot partner list.  
**Exit gate:** a clearly defined urgent workflow, an attainable verification method, and evidence that nurses and employers will participate.

### Phase 1 — Product and system design (Weeks 5–8; indicative)
**Work:** domain model, permissions matrix, UX prototype, API contracts, data classification, audit design, threat model, operational procedures.  
**Deliverables:** architecture decision records, prototype, schema, API specification, test strategy, privacy notice draft, support/moderation playbooks.  
**Exit gate:** engineering and domain stakeholders approve the scope and security model.

### Phase 2 — Trust-first MVP (Weeks 9–16; indicative)
**Work:** accounts, profiles, verification workflow, employer onboarding, controlled search/contact, reporting, admin tools, observability, deployment automation.  
**Deliverables:** tested MVP, runbooks, backup/restore proof, security findings report, synthetic test dataset.  
**Exit gate:** no unresolved critical/high security issues; access-control tests pass; manual review operations are feasible.

### Phase 3 — Closed pilot (Weeks 17–22; indicative)
**Work:** onboard pilot nurses/employers, support workflows, measure verification time and successful connections, respond to abuse.  
**Deliverables:** pilot report, user feedback, operational cost estimates, prioritized defect list.  
**Exit gate:** evidence of repeat use, user trust, acceptable verification accuracy, and a plausible paying customer.

### Phase 4 — Jobs marketplace (after pilot gate)
**Work:** job listings, application/contact workflows, moderation and fraud response, employer billing if validated.  
**Exit gate:** sufficient employer demand and reliable abuse-handling capacity.

### Phase 5 — Professional development/community
**Work:** CPD discovery, mentorship, groups, events, moderation tools.  
**Exit gate:** core product retention supports expansion and moderation resources are funded.

### Phase 6 — International mobility
**Work:** destination-specific content and partnerships, carefully scoped recruiter workflows, legal review.  
**Exit gate:** expert-reviewed content, clear limits, compliance readiness, and validated demand in a specific corridor.

Dates are planning estimates, not promises. A phase should not advance simply because its calendar window has ended.

## 18. Quality Assurance and Release Criteria

Test categories:
- Unit tests for domain rules.
- Integration tests for database, file storage, notifications, and verification workflows.
- API contract tests.
- Authorization tests for every role and resource.
- Abuse and rate-limit tests.
- Upload validation and malware-handling tests.
- Accessibility and mobile usability tests.
- Performance tests for search and profile loading.
- Backup/restore and disaster-recovery exercises.
- Dependency and static security scans.
- Human review of verification decisions and appeals.
- Pilot acceptance tests based on actual user tasks.

Release must be blocked by:
- Unauthorized profile/evidence access.
- Exposure of private evidence or secrets.
- Unreliable or misleading verification labels.
- No functioning report/appeal process.
- Unrecoverable data loss or untested backups.
- Critical unresolved security vulnerabilities.
- No incident owner or support route.

## 19. Success Metrics

### North-star outcome
**Legitimate, consent-based professional connections that result in useful career outcomes**, not raw registrations.

### Trust metrics
- Percentage of profiles with clearly stated verification status.
- Verification turnaround time and review cost.
- Appeal rate and reversal rate.
- Confirmed impersonation/fraud incidents.
- Employer legitimacy and job-listing quality.

### Product metrics
- Profile completion and update rate.
- Search-to-contact conversion.
- Nurse response rate.
- Repeat employer use.
- Time from employer onboarding to first qualified contact.
- Monthly active nurses and employers, interpreted alongside quality.

### Safety metrics
- Reports per 1,000 active users.
- Time to acknowledge and resolve serious reports.
- Unauthorized access incidents.
- Scraping/abuse events blocked.
- User privacy complaints.
- Data deletion and access requests completed within policy.

### Business metrics
- Paying employers and renewal.
- Revenue per employer.
- Cost per verification.
- Gross margin after support and moderation.
- Customer acquisition cost and payback period.

- Product lead: scope, discovery, prioritization, metrics.
- Nursing/domain lead: workflows, terminology, stakeholder engagement.
- Technical lead/architect: system boundaries, technical decisions, reliability.
- Backend engineer: API, domain rules, access control, data.
- Frontend engineer: responsive user journeys and accessibility.

- Community/moderation lead: employer vetting, reports, abuse response.

## 22. Key Decisions Required from the Team

Before implementation begins, the team should explicitly decide:
1. Which exact nurse segment and employer segment will be served first?
2. What evidence can be verified lawfully and reliably at launch?
3. What does each verification label mean, and what does it not mean?
4. What is public by default, and what is private by default?
5. What is the minimum viable profile?
6. What is the first paid workflow and who has budget authority?
7. Which country/career pathways are explicitly excluded from the initial release?
8. What are the operational service levels for verification, appeals, and reports?
9. What hosting, database, object storage, messaging, and monitoring choices fit the cost and security requirements?
10. What are the release-blocking security and trust criteria?
11. Who is accountable for verification operations and incident response?
12. What evidence will cause the team to stop, pivot, or expand?

## 24. Final Recommendation

Proceed to a **time-boxed validation phase**, not an unrestricted feature build. Nurse.ng has a credible product direction if it solves a specific trust-and-career workflow better than the current alternatives. Its most important asset will not be the number of profiles; it will be the accuracy and transparency of its claims, the quality of participating employers, and the value it creates for nurses.

The team should build the smallest trustworthy service that proves a nurse can maintain a useful profile and connect with a legitimate employer. Expand only when demand, verification reliability, safety operations, and unit economics justify it.

## 26. Approved Community Hardening Plan — 28 October 2026

An initial implementation now exists in the working tree for the public feed, shared topic conversations, text/image/video posts, external video links, replies, nurse discovery, following, accepted connections, reports, and staff moderation. This section records the hardening choices approved after inspecting that implementation. Completion and verification results must be recorded separately; approval of the plan is not a claim that every item already works.

### Trust boundaries

- A document-review approval stores the display name actually reviewed. If the profile name changes later, the public receipt distinguishes the reviewed name from the current self-declared name.
- A published discoverable profile must keep its display name public. A member who wants to hide that name must make the profile private first.
- Public community names are case-insensitively unique, reserve regulator/platform/staff-like identities, and lock after the member's first post or reply. The name remains self-chosen, not professional verification.

### Initial abuse and storage controls

- Per account: up to 10 post attempts per hour, 60 reply attempts per hour, and 20 report attempts per hour.
- Upload accounting: up to 500 MB per 24-hour account window and 5 GB retained per account, alongside the existing maximum of four images and one three-minute uploaded video per post.
- Uploads are streamed to temporary files in bounded chunks rather than loaded wholly into application memory.
- Removing a reported post cleans up its public media and releases retained-storage quota. Operator reconciliation handles filesystem/database crash gaps.
- ClamAV scanning is optional for the pilot: use it when available and always reject a positive detection, but log and allow the upload if the scanner is absent or fails. This fail-open choice is not production-ready.

### Relationship and moderation integrity

- A canonical unordered connection key prevents reciprocal duplicate connection rows while preserving who requested and who must respond.
- A unique open-report key prevents duplicate concurrent reports from the same member against the same contribution.
- Conditional updates ensure that only the first concurrent connection response or moderation decision succeeds.

### Delivery and history boundary

The implementation must be covered by focused tests and then by the complete test, lint, dependency, and migration verification set. Work is split into logical local commits. The builder explicitly retained responsibility for reconciling and publishing the patch-equivalent local and remote branch histories; no merge, rebase, or push is part of this implementation task.
