Nurse.ng Build Plan

A review of the strategic roadmap, the stack decisions it still needs, and a 22-week path to a trust-first MVP that a small team can actually operate.

The strategy is disciplined and the scope is right. The build risk sits in one place: whether professional status can be verified lawfully, and reviewed at a cost the business can carry. This plan makes that a Phase 0 kill criterion before verification code is written.

01

Review

What the proposal gets right, and the ten changes that should happen before implementation spending begins.

What holds up

  • Trust-first scope. Profiles, verification, and controlled employer contact come before jobs, CPD, and mobility. The sequencing follows operational maturity, not feature ambition.
  • Explicit non-goals. Not a regulator, not a licence issuer, no patient records, no unmoderated messaging. These prevent the most common failure modes in this category.
  • A real verification taxonomy. Self-declared, document reviewed, source-confirmed, expired, disputed, and unable to verify is already better than most marketplaces.
  • Exit gates and a risk register. Rare in a product proposal. The structure is correct; the thresholds are missing.
  • Privacy designed in. Field-level visibility, data minimisation, appeal rights, and separation of public profiles from private evidence are treated as product requirements.
  • Users separated from payers. The document refuses to charge nurses for basic discoverability, which protects the supply side.

What must change before build

Change 01

Make the source a kill gate

Does NMCN expose a lawful verification route? Can any authoritative source be queried reliably? If the answer is documents only, every label, price, and claim changes. Decide this before schema work.

Change 02

Price the review labour

Time ten real cases. Measure minutes per case, queue SLA, and appeal rate. Cost per verified profile is loaded reviewer cost divided by cases per reviewer per day. If that does not fit under a plausible employer price, the model fails.

Change 03

Name the legal regime

Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023: registration where applicable, DPIA, lawful basis, data subject rights, cross-border transfers, and breach notification. Counsel in Phase 0, not launch week.

Change 04

Turn gates into numbers

“Evidence of repeat use” is not a gate. Agree thresholds in Phase 0 and publish them internally. The proposals are in section 07.

Change 05

Start invite-only

Public employer search is the largest abuse surface and the hardest operation to staff. A curated cohort and 5 to 8 verified employers proves the workflow without opening the scrape surface.

Change 06

Ship a verification receipt

A badge is not trust. Every public “verified” claim needs a page stating what was checked, against which source, by whom, when, and what it does not mean. Product requirement, not a footnote.

Change 07

Baseline the competition

The roadmap says to test against competitors but names none. Document the register, job boards, associations and WhatsApp groups, agencies, and international pathway firms, then name the one workflow you serve better.

Change 08

Fix the team and timeline math

Eight weeks to MVP is achievable with three engineers and a narrowed scope. With two, it is not, unless verification and moderation run on spreadsheets. Staff the plan or cut the scope.

Change 09

Build the trust surface

Name the operating entity, a physical address, a grievance officer, response SLAs, and a status page. Nurses will upload identity documents. Anonymity is a conversion killer.

Change 10

Keep one source of truth

Treat the Markdown as canonical and generate the PDF in CI. Two hand-maintained copies will drift, and the drift will land in front of a funder or a lawyer.

Also add to the roadmap

Market baseline, verification-source decision memo, retention schedule, incident response and breach communications, a WhatsApp support model, an accessibility and performance budget, and a data-processing register with vendor DPAs.

03

Architecture

One Django web application, one Celery worker, one Celery Beat scheduler, one relational database, one private object store. No microservices until organisational boundaries justify them.

Evidence path

  • A Django view issues a short-lived presigned upload URL.
  • The file lands in the quarantine prefix. The view records SHA-256, MIME type, size, and uploader.
  • A Celery task scans the object, rejects malware, re-encodes images where applicable, and moves accepted files to the clean prefix.
  • The reviewer console requests a five-minute download URL. The read is audited.
  • A decision row is created, and the claim plus public projection update in one transaction.

No path exists from the public profile to an evidence object. That is a testable invariant, not a policy statement.

Boundary rules

  • Views compose; services decide. views.py handles HTTP and template context; services.py holds business rules; selectors.py holds query logic. No business rules in templates.
  • Apps own their models and migrations. Cross-app access goes through services or selectors, not another app’s models directly.
  • Authorize at the boundary. Every view and HTMX action calls authorize(actor, action, resource), never only in the template.
  • Public reads use projections. Public profile pages use a dedicated selector. They never load evidence or private fields.
  • Evidence stays opaque. Storage keys are never returned to clients; signed URLs are minted server-side only.
  • Tasks stay framework-free. Celery tasks import services, never views or templates.
  • Forms are adapters. Domain validation lives in services or shared validators. Do not duplicate rules between forms, API serializers, and tasks.
  • Templates escape by default. Any mark_safe or |safe requires review.
  • Custom user model from day one. Use identity.User from the first migration. Never use auth.User directly.
  • Uploads are hostile. Enforce MIME, size, extension, and scan status before any processing.
05

Data & trust model

The trust model is a small set of invariants enforced by the schema and the authorization layer, not by careful UI. The two most important: a claim cannot become verified without a decision row, and evidence has no public path.

Before the first migration

Use a custom user model, identity.User, from the first migration. Changing it after data exists is painful, and Django’s built-in auth.User is not suitable for phone-first accounts or role assignment.

Core invariants

  • IDs are UUIDv7. A licence or registration number is never a primary key or a public identifier.
  • VerificationDecision and AuditEvent are append-only. Corrections are new rows.
  • Field-level visibility defaults to private. Public fields are explicitly opted in.
  • Search returns only profiles where discoverable = true. Contact details never appear in search results.
  • Account deletion follows a documented workflow: revoke sessions, remove the public projection, retain audit records per policy, delete evidence on schedule.
  • Every case transition writes an audit event in the same transaction as the state change.

Capacity check

The six build sprints (S2–S7) total 112 engineer-days; pilot tooling adds 25 more during weeks 17–22. Three engineers across the twelve build weeks provide 180 person-days, leaving roughly 38% for review, defects, and coordination. Two engineers provide 120 days, roughly 7% buffer, which is effectively none. Staff three engineers, or cut scope to a single employer segment and manual verification.

Phase 0 technical spikes

  • Verification source. What NMCN exposes publicly, what lawful access exists, what documents nurses can provide, and what a reviewer can reasonably check.
  • SMS and identity. Termii and Twilio deliverability, sender ID approval, and phone verification fallback.
  • Low-bandwidth baseline. Measure a profile page on a mid-range Android over simulated 3G, then set the budget before UI work.
  • Reviewer console shape. Timebox a comparison of customized Django admin against a dedicated reviewer view for evidence preview, decisions, and audit. Pick one before S4.

Gates & budgets

Proposed thresholds, to be agreed in Phase 0. A phase does not advance because its calendar window ended.

Phase 0 Decide

  • Verification route documented, with a named fallback and legal sign-off.
  • 15 nurse and 5 employer interviews completed.
  • Pilot commitments from at least 3 employers.
  • Numeric pilot gates agreed.

Phase 1 Foundation

  • Authorization matrix 100% green.
  • Threat model signed off.
  • Storage test proves no public read path.
  • Schema reviewed; migrations reversible.
  • CI green; restore drill passed.

Phase 2 MVP

  • Zero unresolved critical or high security findings.
  • p95 candidate search under 800 ms at pilot scale.
  • Profile LCP under 3 s on simulated 3G; initial JS under 100 KB gzipped.
  • Every high-impact action produces an audit event.
  • Pen-test findings closed or formally accepted.
  • Runbooks exercised by someone other than their author.

Phase 3 Pilot

  • 40+ nurses onboarded, 5+ employers active.
  • Median verification decision within 3 business days.
  • 20+ consent-based contacts; 25%+ nurse response rate.
  • 3+ employers willing to pay or sign a paid pilot.
  • Cost per verified profile measured and inside target.
  • Zero reportable data incidents.

Performance budget

  • Initial JavaScript on public profile pages: at most 100 KB gzipped, including HTMX and app modules.
  • No SPA framework in the MVP. Server-rendered pages with HTMX partials come first.
  • LCP at most 3 seconds on a mid-range Android over Slow 4G.
  • TTFB from Lagos at most 800 ms.
  • No web font blocks first render; system fallback first.
  • Public profile and sign-in degrade gracefully without JavaScript.

Cost model to instrument from day one

  • Minutes per verification case, and cases per reviewer per day.
  • Cost per verified profile, including scanning and storage.
  • Support and moderation minutes per active user.
  • Employer acquisition cost and revenue per paying employer.
  • Fraud and refund cost per listing.
  • Gross margin after verification and moderation.
